Lao labour law — principally the Labour Law (No. 43/NA, 2013, as of July 2026) — sets the ground rules for the relationship between employers and employees in the Lao PDR: how employment contracts are formed, what working conditions must be respected, how employment ends, and what protections employees have. It applies to Lao and foreign employees working in Laos, and to Lao and foreign-owned employers alike. The Ministry of Labour and Social Welfare administers the framework, alongside the social security scheme that covers registered employees.

## Who it affects

- Every employer in Laos, from a shop with one employee to a large foreign-invested company
- Lao employees, whose rights and protections the law defines
- Foreign employees, who are covered by the labour framework and additionally by work permit and quota rules
- Company managers and HR staff, who carry the practical responsibility for compliance

## Key obligations

The details vary by situation, but employers in Laos are commonly expected to:

- Put employment contracts in writing, in a form consistent with the labour rules, and keep signed copies
- Respect the legal limits on working hours, rest days, overtime, and public holidays, and compensate overtime as the rules require
- Pay at least the official minimum wage, which is revised from time to time — confirm the current level before setting pay
- Register employees with the social security scheme and remit employer and employee contributions
- Follow the rules on probation, leave entitlements (annual, sick, maternity), and workplace safety
- Follow the lawful procedures for discipline and termination, including required notice and any severance the rules provide — termination handled informally is a frequent source of disputes
- Respect quota and approval rules when hiring foreign employees, and support their work permit applications

Minimum wage levels, contribution rates, leave entitlements, and notice periods are set by regulation and revised periodically — confirm the current figures with the labour authority before acting on them.

## Common mistakes

- Hiring on a handshake with no written contract, leaving both sides exposed when a dispute arises
- Copying a foreign-template employment contract that conflicts with mandatory Lao rules
- Skipping social security registration for some or all staff
- Terminating employees without following the procedure, then facing claims for reinstatement or compensation
- Employing foreign staff before their work permits are in place

## Where to confirm the current rules

The Ministry of Labour and Social Welfare and the social security organisation are the authorities for current requirements, rates, and procedures.
