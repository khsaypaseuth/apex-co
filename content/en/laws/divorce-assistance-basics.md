Lao family law provides for the dissolution of a registered marriage, and the route depends mainly on whether the spouses agree. Where both spouses consent to the divorce and to the practical arrangements, the process is generally administrative and simpler. Where one spouse does not agree, or the couple cannot settle property or child arrangements, the matter typically goes through the court, often after an attempt at mediation — Lao practice places real weight on reconciliation and mediation before a marriage is dissolved.

## Who it affects

- Married couples in Laos seeking to end a registered marriage, whether both are Lao or one spouse is a foreign national
- Foreign spouses, whose stay permits or visa status may be linked to the marriage and can be affected by its dissolution
- Children of the marriage, for whom custody and support arrangements must be settled
- Couples married abroad, whose divorce may raise questions about which country's authorities should handle it and how the outcome is recognised in Laos

## What typically must be resolved

A divorce is not only the end of the marriage but a settlement of everything built on it. The process commonly needs to address:

- The legal ground and route — mutual consent through the administrative path, or a contested case through the court
- Division of marital property, which under Lao law generally distinguishes between property acquired during the marriage and property each spouse held before it — land raises particular issues where one spouse is foreign
- Custody, care, and financial support of children
- Formal registration of the divorce, so that civil records reflect it — an unregistered separation leaves both spouses legally married, with all the consequences that carries
- For foreign spouses, the immigration consequences, since a stay permit based on marriage may need to be replaced with another basis

Procedures, required documents, and fees vary by case and district — confirm the current requirements with the authority or court handling the matter.

## Common mistakes

- Separating informally for years without registering a divorce, then discovering the marriage still binds property, inheritance, and remarriage plans
- Agreeing property or custody arrangements verbally with nothing recorded in the settlement
- Overlooking the immigration consequences for a foreign spouse until a renewal is refused
- Assuming a divorce obtained abroad automatically has effect in Laos without any recognition step
- Going into a contested case without organised documentation of property, contributions, and the children's situation
