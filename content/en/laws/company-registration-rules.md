Lao law requires businesses operating in the Lao PDR to be registered with the authorities before they begin trading. The rules governing enterprise registration set out which legal forms a business may take, how a company name is reserved and approved, what founding documents are required, and which authority handles the registration. In practice, most registrations are handled through the enterprise registration offices under the Ministry of Industry and Commerce, with additional steps at other agencies depending on the business activity.

> **Needs legal verification before publication.**

## Who it affects

The registration rules affect anyone carrying on a business activity in Laos, including:

- Lao citizens starting a sole trader business or a company
- Foreign investors establishing a wholly foreign-owned company or a joint venture
- Existing foreign companies opening a branch or representative office
- Businesses changing their structure, shareholders, registered address, or business activities, since changes typically must be reported and re-registered

Some activities are reserved, conditional, or restricted for foreign ownership. Whether a planned activity is open to foreign investment — and under what conditions — should always be confirmed against the current controlled business list before committing to a structure.

## Key obligations

Exact requirements vary by legal form and activity, but registered companies in Laos are commonly expected to:

- Register the enterprise and obtain an enterprise registration certificate before starting operations
- Register the company name in Lao language form and use it consistently on official documents
- Maintain a registered office address in Laos
- Complete tax registration and obtain the relevant tax identification once registered
- Obtain any operating licenses that the specific business activity requires — enterprise registration alone does not authorise every activity
- Report changes to shareholders, directors, capital, address, or activities to the registration authority within the timeframes the authority sets
- Keep company documents, accounts, and records as required by the accounting and tax rules

Minimum registered capital expectations, official fees, and processing timeframes vary by activity and are updated from time to time — confirm the current figures with the registration authority before filing.

## Common mistakes

- Starting to trade — signing contracts, hiring staff, invoicing — before the registration certificate is issued
- Assuming the enterprise registration certificate covers everything, and skipping the separate operating licenses the activity requires
- Choosing a company structure without checking foreign ownership conditions for the planned activity
- Letting the registered details drift out of date — an old address or an unreported shareholder change can block later filings
- Losing track of the follow-on obligations, especially tax registration and annual compliance, which begin as soon as the company exists

## Where to confirm the current rules

The enterprise registration offices of the Ministry of Industry and Commerce are the primary authority for company registration questions. The Lao Trade Portal publishes procedural information in English. As of July 2026, registered enterprise names and basic company details can also be searched in the ministry's National Enterprise Database. Because forms, fees, and requirements change, always confirm the current requirements directly with the authority — or ask Super Consulting to confirm them for your specific case.
