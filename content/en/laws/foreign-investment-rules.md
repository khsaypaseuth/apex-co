Foreign investment in the Lao PDR is governed primarily by the investment promotion framework — the Law on Investment Promotion as amended, in force since 1 October 2024 — which sets out how foreign individuals and companies may invest, which sectors are open, conditional, or reserved, and what incentives may be available for promoted activities. The framework distinguishes between general business activities, which typically register through the ordinary enterprise registration channel, and concession activities — such as land concessions, mining, or energy projects — which follow a separate approval process with additional government agreements.

> **Needs legal verification before publication.**

## Who it affects

- Foreign individuals or companies planning to establish a business in Laos, alone or with Lao partners
- Foreign investors acquiring shares in an existing Lao company
- Investors in concession-based sectors, which involve negotiated agreements with the government
- Foreign investors seeking investment incentives, special economic zone status, or long-term stay arrangements linked to their investment

Certain activities are reserved for Lao citizens or subject to foreign ownership caps, minimum capital conditions, or joint venture requirements. The controlled and reserved lists are updated periodically, so what was open last year may carry conditions today — always check the current lists.

## Key obligations

Requirements depend on the sector and investment form, but foreign investors are commonly expected to:

- Confirm that the planned activity is open to foreign investment, and under what conditions, before structuring the deal
- Register the enterprise and, where relevant, obtain sector approvals before operating
- Bring in and register the committed investment capital through the proper channels, typically evidenced through a Lao bank account, and be able to document it
- Comply with reporting obligations to the investment and registration authorities, which commonly include periodic reports on investment progress
- Meet the conditions attached to any incentives received — incentives are typically conditional and can be withdrawn if conditions are not met
- Follow the tax, labour, and immigration rules that apply to the operating business, including work permits for foreign staff

Minimum capital figures, incentive rates, and concession terms vary by sector and change over time — confirm the current position with the investment authority before relying on them.

## Common mistakes

- Signing agreements or paying deposits before confirming the activity is open to foreign ownership
- Using informal nominee arrangements to sidestep restrictions — these create serious legal risk for both sides
- Failing to properly document the transfer of investment capital into Laos, which can create problems later with profit repatriation and compliance checks
- Treating incentive commitments as automatic rather than conditional
- Overlooking the day-to-day compliance obligations — tax filings, reporting, work permits — that follow the initial approval

## Where to confirm the current rules

As of July 2026, investment promotion is overseen by the Investment Promotion and Management Committee (IPMC), whose office serves as the central one-stop investment service and publishes official information for investors through the Invest Laos portal. Sector ministries handle activity-specific approvals. Because sector lists and conditions change, confirm the current requirements for your specific activity with the authority — or ask Super Consulting to assess your planned investment.
