Registering a company in Laos is a one-time event; keeping it compliant is a yearly rhythm. Most compliance failures are not dramatic — they are quiet lapses: a licence that expired, a filing that slipped, a register that was never updated. This checklist organises the recurring obligations most companies face so you can build a calendar around them. Exact deadlines and requirements are set by regulation and change, so each item should be confirmed for your company and the current year.

> **Needs legal verification before publication.**

## Tax and accounting

- **Periodic tax filings.** File every return on its cycle — including nil returns where required. Confirm the current filing calendar with the tax authority.
- **Annual tax finalisation.** Prepare year-end financial statements and the annual profit tax return, and settle any balance by the deadline in force.
- **VAT compliance**, where registered: returns on cycle, invoices in the required form, records reconciled to filings.
- **Payroll obligations.** Salary tax withholding and social security contributions for every employee, remitted on the required cycle.
- **Books and records.** Maintain accounts to Lao requirements and keep supporting documents organised — deductibility and audits both depend on them.

## Licences and registrations

- **Operating licence renewals.** List every licence the company holds, note each expiry date, and start renewals early.
- **Enterprise registration accuracy.** If the address, shareholders, directors, capital, or activities have changed, the registration should be updated — stale registry information causes problems with banks, authorities, and counterparties.
- **Sector-specific reporting.** Some supervised sectors require periodic reports to their supervising authority; confirm whether yours does.

## Labour and employment

- **Employment contracts** in place and current for all staff.
- **Work permits and visas** for foreign employees — each with its own expiry date and renewal lead time.
- **Social security registration** covering all eligible employees, including recent hires.
- **Labour reporting obligations**, where applicable, confirmed and filed.

## Corporate housekeeping

- **Shareholder and director records** kept up to date, with decisions documented.
- **Company documents** — certificates, licences, tax documents, leases — stored where they can actually be produced on request.
- **Contracts register.** Know what the company has signed, what renews automatically, and what expires this year.

## A simple way to run this

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Start of year | Build the compliance calendar: every filing, renewal, and expiry with owner and lead time |
| Each filing cycle | Prepare from the books, file, and archive proof of filing |
| Mid-year | Review licences, permits, and registry accuracy — catch drift early |
| Before year end | Pre-close the accounts so finalisation is an exercise, not an excavation |

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a quiet company has no obligations — dormant companies generally still have filing duties
- Tracking tax deadlines but not licence and permit expiries
- Leaving the whole year's bookkeeping to finalisation time
- Nobody owning the calendar — compliance that is everyone's job is no one's job
