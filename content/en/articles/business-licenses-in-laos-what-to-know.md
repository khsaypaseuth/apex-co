Registering a company and being licensed to operate are two different things in Laos. The enterprise registration certificate establishes the company as a legal entity, but many activities also require an operating licence, permit, or approval from the ministry or authority that supervises that sector. Understanding which licences apply to your activity — before you commit to premises, staff, or contracts — is one of the most valuable pieces of early planning.

## Registration vs licensing

A useful way to think about it:

| Step | What it does | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise registration | Creates the legal entity | The enterprise registration authority |
| Tax registration | Puts the company into the tax system | The tax authority |
| Operating licence(s) | Permits the specific regulated activity | The supervising ministry or authority for that sector |

Not every business needs a separate operating licence — some general activities can operate on the strength of registration alone — but many common sectors are supervised, and operating without a required licence is a compliance risk.

## Activities that are commonly licensed

The exact list is set by regulation and changes over time, but sectors that commonly involve additional licences or approvals include:

- Food and beverage, restaurants, and hospitality
- Tourism and travel services
- Import, export, and distribution of regulated goods
- Construction and engineering services
- Education and training
- Health-related services and pharmaceuticals
- Financial services
- Transport and logistics

If your activity touches any supervised sector, assume a licence question exists and confirm it early. The Lao Trade Portal publishes information about trade-related permits and procedures, which is a useful starting point for import/export activities.

## How the licensing process generally works

1. **Identify the supervising authority** for the activity.
2. **Confirm the current requirements** — application forms, supporting documents, premises or qualification conditions.
3. **Prepare the application**, aligning it with the company's registered activity description.
4. **Submit and respond to questions** from the authority, including inspections where the sector requires them.
5. **Receive the licence and diarise renewal.** Many licences are time-limited and must be renewed; the renewal cycle should be confirmed for each licence you hold.

## Common mistakes

- **Operating while "registration is enough".** In supervised sectors, registration without the sector licence is not sufficient.
- **Mismatch between the registered activity and the licence applied for.** Authorities compare them; inconsistencies cause rejections and delays.
- **Missing renewals.** Licences lapse quietly; a renewal calendar is a simple fix.
- **Scaling into new activities without new licences.** Adding a new line of business often means adding a new licence.

## Costs and processing times

Licence fees and processing periods differ by sector and authority, and current rates should be confirmed with the relevant authority — published figures go stale quickly, so we do not repeat them here.
