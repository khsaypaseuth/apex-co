Value added tax (VAT) is a consumption tax applied to most goods and services in Laos. Businesses that are registered for VAT charge it on their sales, may credit the VAT they pay on their purchases, and account for the difference to the tax authority. This article explains the concept and the compliance process in plain language — it deliberately does not state rates or thresholds, which must be confirmed with the tax authority.

> **Needs legal verification before publication.**

## How VAT works in principle

VAT is designed to tax final consumption while remaining broadly neutral for businesses in the chain:

1. A VAT-registered business **adds VAT to its sales invoices** (output VAT).
2. It **pays VAT on its own purchases** from other registered businesses (input VAT).
3. At each filing period, it reports both and **accounts for the difference** — paying the excess of output over input VAT, or carrying forward or reclaiming an excess of input VAT, according to the rules in force.

The economic burden falls on the final consumer; the business acts as a collector.

## Who it affects

- **Businesses above the registration threshold** are generally required to register for VAT. The current threshold should be confirmed with the tax authority.
- **Smaller businesses** may fall outside mandatory registration; whether voluntary registration is possible or sensible depends on the rules in force and on who your customers are.
- **Importers** commonly encounter VAT at the border, collected as part of the import process.
- **Some goods and services** may be exempt or treated specially — the current lists should be confirmed rather than assumed.

## What compliance generally involves

| Task | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Registration | Registering for VAT with the tax authority when required |
| Compliant invoicing | Issuing invoices in the required form, showing VAT correctly |
| Records | Keeping purchase and sales records that support every figure filed |
| Periodic returns | Filing VAT returns and paying on the cycle set by the authority |
| Reconciliation | Making sure the VAT filed matches the accounting records |

The filing cycle and the required invoice form are set by regulation and should be confirmed as part of your setup.

## Common mistakes

- **Charging VAT without being registered** — or failing to register once the business grows past the threshold.
- **Poor invoice discipline.** Input VAT credits generally depend on holding proper invoices; informal receipts can mean losing the credit.
- **Treating VAT collected as cash flow.** Output VAT belongs to the tax authority; spending it creates a hole at filing time.
- **Missing the interaction with imports.** VAT paid at the border needs to flow into the same records and returns as everything else.

## Rates and thresholds

Laos applies VAT at rates set by law, and both rates and registration thresholds have changed over time. We do not publish figures here — current rates and thresholds should be confirmed with the tax authority or a qualified adviser before pricing, invoicing, or registration decisions are made.
