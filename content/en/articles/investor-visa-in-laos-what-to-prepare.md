Foreign nationals who invest in a Lao business — as shareholders, directors, or founders — generally rely on an investor-category visa and associated stay documentation rather than a standard work arrangement. The common thread is that the immigration status is tied to a genuine, documented investment: the stronger and cleaner your company paperwork, the smoother the visa side tends to be.

As of July 2026, the category investors commonly use is the business visa (coded NI-B2 or I-B2) under the Law on Immigration and Foreigner Management, which is issued to foreign business people who come for investment, including their family members. Stay permit cards are issued by the foreigner-management police under the Ministry of Public Security, and investors and developers in project or concession agreements with the Government of the Lao PDR can receive stay permit cards valid from one to five years, renewable.

## Who this is for

- Founders and shareholders of Lao-registered companies
- Directors and representatives of foreign-invested enterprises
- Investors under promoted investment projects

Whether a specific person qualifies as an "investor" for immigration purposes — and how that differs from an employee needing a work permit — depends on the current rules and the person's actual role, and should be confirmed case by case. Directors who also perform day-to-day work may face work-permit questions in addition to visa ones.

## What the process generally involves

1. **Establish or document the investment.** The company registration, capital contribution, and any investment licence come first — the visa follows the investment, not the other way round.
2. **Obtain the appropriate visa category** for entry, where required, before switching to or applying for longer-term documentation.
3. **Apply for the longer-term stay documentation** tied to the investor role, on the cycle and duration set by the current rules.
4. **Renew on time.** Investor documentation is time-limited; renewal generally requires showing the investment and company remain active and compliant.

The order of steps and the authorities involved should be confirmed at the time of application, as procedures are updated periodically.

## Documents commonly prepared

| Document | Why it is requested |
| --- | --- |
| Passport with sufficient validity | Identity and immigration processing |
| Enterprise registration certificate | Evidence the invested company exists |
| Evidence of shareholding or capital contribution | Ties the applicant to the investment |
| Company tax registration and compliance documents | Shows the company is genuinely operating |
| Photographs and application forms | Standard processing requirements |

Additional documents can be requested depending on the sector, the size of the investment, and whether the project is under investment promotion. Confirm the current checklist before assembling the file.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying before the investment paperwork is complete.** Gaps in the company file surface as visa delays.
- **A dormant company behind the visa.** Renewals get difficult when the company has stopped filing taxes or renewing licences — immigration status and corporate compliance are linked in practice.
- **Confusing investor status with permission to work for other companies.** The status attaches to your investment, not to the labour market generally.
- **Leaving renewals late.** Processing times vary; a lapsed status is far harder to fix than a renewed one.
